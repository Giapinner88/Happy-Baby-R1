# T007 — Live/replay equivalence and arm/head simulation pilot

## Folded-posture local minimum — 2026-08-18

`t007_whole_upper_body_20260818T114338Z` ran with the corrected head convention
and `body_mode=arms_head`: head tracking error fell to 0.002 rad mean, no target
was refused, and the rate rose to 9.98 Hz. It still showed a wrong elbow
whenever the operator drew the hands in towards the body.

That is a solver failure, not a reach limit. Binned by distance from the
shoulder, targets 0.20–0.30 m away — well inside the arm, which can fold to
0.152 m and extend to 0.510 m — carried a **0.203 m mean position residual**
while `shoulder_yaw` and `elbow` barely moved (spans of 3° and 5°). Further out
the residual was smaller.

The cause is the continuation seed. Re-solving the worst such sample:

| Seed | Position residual |
|---|---|
| previous solution (as live) | 0.2068 m |
| declared nominal | 0.0297 m |
| best of 40 random seeds | 0.0129 m |

A good solution exists; the extended previous posture simply cannot cross into
the folded basin. Raising `max_iterations` to 400, enlarging
`max_joint_step_rad`, lowering `damping`, and weakening the wrist-orientation
weight by 100× all returned **exactly 0.2068 m at iteration 31** — none of them
changes the basin, only the seed does.

The sink now re-solves once from the declared nominal when a solve stalls above
`seed_restart_residual_m` **and** both targets lie inside the arms' reach bound.
That bound is derived from the URDF by triangle inequality over the link
translations plus the tool offset (0.5828 m against a 0.5584 m sampled maximum),
so it is conservative by construction rather than a tuned constant. The gate
matters: without it the restart also fires on genuinely out-of-reach targets and
costs 261.7 ms per target, while with it the cost is unchanged.

Replaying the run's 297 targets through the sink, changing only this:

| | close-in mean | close-in max | all mean | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 0.3815 m | 0.5943 m | 0.2788 m | 157.0 ms |
| after | **0.0010 m** | **0.0025 m** | 0.0762 m | 162.5 ms |

Only 10 restarts fired across 297 targets. Their value is disproportionate
because one trapped solve poisons the seed for every frame after it.

The exact failing sample is pinned as a regression in
`tests/teleop/test_r1_upper_body_ik.py` as literals, so it does not depend on
the evidence directory.

This is offline replay of a recorded trace, not a new live run. Achieved rate,
smoothness and post-physics tracking under the restart remain unassessed, and
the run's own `scientific_outcome` stays `unassessed`.

## Head-target convention defect and body modes — 2026-08-18

`t007_whole_upper_body_20260818T072252Z` recorded a body pose that did not
follow the operator while hands and head tracked acceptably, and ran at
**8.57 Hz against a requested 20 Hz**. Both symptoms trace to one defect.

The sink built the head target as `Rz(yaw) @ Ry(pitch)`, but the R1 head chain
applies `head_pitch` before `head_yaw`. The two orders differ whenever both
angles are non-zero, so the head target was **not realizable** by the two head
joints. Consequences, measured from that run's evidence:

- **0 of 334** dispatched targets converged (the earlier run: 687/687).
- Mean solver iterations rose from **2.4 to 34.5** against a cap of 40, so IK
  cost **297 ms** per solving step versus 55.5 ms before, against a 50 ms
  budget. IK consumed 56.9% of wall time; steps without IK held 50.4 ms, i.e.
  the loop was otherwise correctly sized for 20 Hz. This is the slowdown.
- Chasing the unsatisfiable head orientation, the solver recruited
  `waist_yaw` — range ±150°, opposed only by a null-space posture bias 200×
  weaker than the position task — which ratcheted across frames from 17° to a
  **108.4°** twist. This is the body-pose symptom.

The run's operator turned their head far more than the previous one (mean
|yaw| 67.8° versus 11.2°, both angles above 10° on 17.1% of samples), which is
why the defect surfaced then and not earlier. The earlier run is not evidence
that the convention was correct.

The head target is now derived from the head chain's own FK. Offline replay of
the same 334 targets, with no other change, gives |waist_yaw| max **10.1°**
(from 108.4°) and mean head residual **0.0137 rad** (from 0.1199).

Two further findings are recorded rather than tuned around:

- Solution quality is **not monotonic** in the iteration budget. On this trace
  12 and 40 iterations both behave (|waist_yaw| max ≈ 10°), while 20 diverges
  to 136.8°. The iteration cap is therefore not a safe tuning dial, and the
  40-iteration cap is retained.
- The second evidence camera added in the same change costs ~1.9 ms per step
  (48.5 → 50.4 ms) and is not a material contributor to the rate.

A `body_mode` selector now chooses which torso joints the solver owns:
`arms_head` (12 DoF, torso frozen), `waist_yaw` (13, hardware-common) or
`full_upper_body` (14, adds waist roll as a simulation-only deviation).
`arms_head` removes the waist from the task, so it cannot be recruited at all;
`make teleop BODY_MODE=arms_head` selects it without editing the profile.

All prior schema-3 runs remain immutable legacy evidence and are **not**
comparable with runs made after this correction. No live run has yet been made
with the corrected convention: the figures above are offline replay of a
recorded target trace, not new Quest evidence, and tracking, smoothness and
achieved rate remain unassessed on hardware or in a fresh live session.

## Boundary projection, waist-roll option and one-command launch — 2026-08-18

`t007_whole_upper_body_20260818T050419Z` completed but refused 133 of its 820
enabled targets with `upper_body_ik_refused:iteration_budget_exhausted`, most
repeatedly clamped at `left_shoulder_roll_joint`. Because the coupled sink held
all 13 joints on any non-converged solve, each refusal froze the whole upper
body, which is what an operator sees as arms that stop extending and a
trajectory that stops being followed.

Three changes follow. All are protocol changes, so this run and every earlier
schema-3 run remain immutable legacy evidence and are not comparable with runs
made after this entry.

1. **Boundary projection.** The coupled solver now returns the closest
   reachable iterate as `projected_to_reachable_boundary`, matching the
   single-arm solver. Its stagnation test is relative rather than an absolute
   `1e-14` score delta, which never fired for far targets. The editable profile
   sets `allow_projected_position_solution: true`, so a projected target is
   dispatched and recorded as `solver_solution_kind: "projected"` with its
   residual instead of freezing every joint. `singular_system` still holds.
2. **Optional waist roll.** `control_waist_roll: true` adds `waist_roll_joint`
   as a 14th controlled joint for torso lean. It is a declared simulation-only
   deviation — the R1-A5 motor interface marks that slot unused — and defaults
   to `false`. See `docs/teleop/r1_upper_body_ik.md`.
3. **Second evidence camera and one-command launch.** `--dual-view` records a
   mirrored second fixed camera and stores both views side by side in one
   synchronized video. `make teleop` (or
   `scripts/teleop/run_t007_upper_body_pilot.py`) replaces the hand-typed
   two-process pipeline below and allocates the run id, stop file and evidence
   directory the same way the T001-B launcher does.

Dispatching those previously-frozen targets exposed a fourth, pre-existing
defect: the second-order rate limiter could carry a command 0.133 rad past
`left_shoulder_roll_joint`'s lower limit when a target reversed direction,
because the acceleration limit cannot cancel stored velocity in one step and
nothing clamped the limiter's output. The coupled sink now passes the model's
joint limits to the limiter, which clamps and zeroes the velocity of clamped
joints. After the fix the same replay dispatches nothing outside any joint
limit. The legacy per-arm sink retains the unclamped limiter; changing it would
alter schema-2 comparability and was left alone.

Offline replay of this run's 820 recorded targets through the revised solver
raises dispatch from 591/820 (72.1%) to 820/820: 491 exact (0.7 mm mean
position residual) and 329 projected (366–429 mm mean). That large residual is
the real finding — the recorded Quest targets reach 0.954 m from the pelvis
against roughly 0.750 m of arm reach, so the operator is reaching outside the
R1 workspace at 1:1 absolute scale and the arm can only extend to its boundary.
Whether to keep 1:1 absolute mapping or introduce a scale is an open protocol
question, not something this change decides.

**Not yet verified:** no live Quest/Isaac run has been made under these
changes. The replay harness measured mean solve time rising from 63.6 ms to
77.3 ms per target with p95 and maximum unchanged, but workstation replay
timing is not comparable with the live `unitree_sim_env` environment. The next
live run must confirm the achieved control rate, the dual-view video, and the
post-physics tracking before any pass claim.

## Schema 3 coupled whole-upper-body pilot — 2026-08-18

The new optional path solves `waist_yaw_joint`, both five-joint arms, and both
head joints in one 13-DoF IK problem. It keeps the root, legs, and
`waist_roll_joint` fixed. This changes the method and joint ownership, so its
runs are not directly comparable with the schema-2 independent-arm evidence
described below. The method is `docs/teleop/r1_upper_body_ik.md`; the editable
pilot configuration is `config/r1_t007_whole_upper_body_live.json`.

Run from the repository root. The launcher allocates the run id, stop file and
evidence directory, and starts both processes:

```bash
make teleop HOST_IP=10.42.0.1
```

`make teleop-dry-run` prints the allocated paths and both underlying commands
without starting anything. The equivalent explicit form is:

```bash
python3 scripts/teleop/run_t007_upper_body_pilot.py --host-ip 10.42.0.1 --duration-s 180
```

The left trigger resets all 13 targets to the declared zero pose; the right
trigger enables fresh input. Stop with `Ctrl-C` and wait for the evidence path
to print. This command has passed CLI/static validation but has not yet produced
a schema-3 Quest/Isaac evidence run; tracking and timing therefore remain
unassessed.

The synthetic smoke
`results/smoke/t007_whole_upper_body_preflight_20260818T000000Z` exercised the
initial schema-3 runner at 20.06 Hz with zero root displacement/velocity and no
base dispatch, but rejected 8 of 26 enabled targets after spending its
iteration budget reducing a secondary null-space posture cost. That exposed a
method error: nominal posture was being treated as a convergence gate even
after wrist/head task tolerances passed. The solver now terminates on the
declared physical task tolerances and keeps posture only as a secondary cost.
Offline replay of the same 26 targets then accepted all 26 in at most 11
iterations (14.0 ms/target mean on this host). The original smoke is retained
as pre-revision diagnostic output and is not evidence for the corrected method;
the corrected simulation smoke is reported next.

The corrected synthetic smoke at
`results/smoke/t007_whole_upper_body_preflight_20260818T044251Z` accepted all
100 enabled targets, sustained 20.06 Hz, dispatched no base command, and kept
root displacement and velocity at exactly zero. Its pure IK position residual
was below 1.9 mm on both arms. This distinguishes the remaining dynamic error
from the geometric solve: requested-to-observed endpoint error was 69.8 mm
left and 83.9 mm right on average, while rate-limited-command-to-observed error
was 57.6/53.4 mm. The largest persistent component in the diagnostic figure is
vertical sag of the simulated articulation under its current actuator model,
not an IK reach failure. The generated unsmoothed figure is
`figures/endpoint_target_vs_achieved.png` inside that smoke directory. This is
still synthetic smoke input, not live Quest evidence or hardware validation.

T007 is now executable as a simulation-only bilateral arm/head pilot. It uses
the same Quest bridge stream as T001-B but, unlike T001, sends the two wrists
through the reviewed 5-DOF IK and drives both arms plus head yaw/pitch. Base
velocity remains hard-disabled. The first deadman-held sample is the session
start marker. Arm positions and wrist roll use the vendor's absolute waist-frame
pose at scale 1:1; the first sample does not redefine an arm neutral.

This controls every direction that the current model can represent: reachable
3-D wrist translation, wrist axial roll, and head yaw/pitch. The current live
profile sends every target to IK first (there is no rectangular workspace
prefilter). Exact reachable targets are solved normally; targets outside the
bounded joint workspace are projected to the closest iterate at the reachable
boundary and marked `projected` with their residual. It does **not** control fingers, wrist pitch/yaw,
torso/waist, balance, or locomotion. A target outside the five-DOF arm's true
reachable set is never approximated by base motion. A malformed solve, stale
input, deadman release or sequence fault freezes the last complete arm/head
target.

T007 now creates a true fixed-base articulation (`fix_root_link=True`), rather
than repeatedly overwriting a free root state. Every arm/head run saves
`dynamics_trace.npz`: root position/velocity, all joint positions/velocities,
and every link's world-frame COM position. Generate diagnostic figures after a
run without altering raw evidence:

```bash
MPLCONFIGDIR=/tmp/mpl conda run --no-capture-output -n r1_env python \
  scripts/teleop/plot_r1_t007_dynamics.py \
  experiments/r1_teleop/quest3_sim_v1/T007/runs/<run-id>
```

For schema-2 runs this also produces `endpoint_target_vs_achieved.png`: raw
Quest virtual-EE targets, FK of the rate-limited joint command, and FK
reconstructed from the post-physics arm joints, plus unsmoothed error
components. Runs made after the 2026-08-17 diagnostics revision additionally
record the pre-limiter IK joint target; older schema-2 runs retain their scalar
IK residual. Black samples are targets the IK explicitly projected to the
reachable boundary. This separates physical workspace mismatch, rate-limit lag
and simulator tracking lag; schema-1 runs are deliberately not reinterpreted
with the new endpoint model.

Cò trái returns both arms and head to joint zero (not the last held pose),
clears the active session, and holds there. Release it, then hold cò phải to
apply the current absolute Quest pose.

The simulation also starts at that same declared joint-zero pose. It no longer
spawns in Isaac's bent-arm default and spends the first seconds rate-limiting an
unrelated transition before following the first Quest target.

Run from repository root with the headset connected. Cò trái is an
edge-triggered reset: it returns the robot to the **declared neutral arm/head
pose**, clears the current session, and requires a fresh current pose on the
next cò phải press. It never reuses the stale pre-disconnect Quest pose.

```bash
run_id="t007_live_arm_head_$(date -u +%Y%m%dT%H%M%SZ)"
conda run --no-capture-output -n tv python scripts/teleop/quest_bridge.py \
  --host-ip 10.42.0.1 --duration-s 180 \
  --trigger-value-threshold 5.0 \
  --connection-log "experiments/r1_teleop/quest3_sim_v1/T007/runs/${run_id}.bridge.jsonl" \
| conda run --no-capture-output -n unitree_sim_env python scripts/teleop/run_r1_quest3_live.py \
  --config experiments/r1_teleop/quest3_sim_v1/T001/config/r1_quest3_sim_v1.json \
  --arm-head-config experiments/r1_teleop/quest3_sim_v1/T007/config/r1_t007_arm_head_live.json \
  --output-dir "experiments/r1_teleop/quest3_sim_v1/T007/runs/${run_id}" \
  --duration-s 180 --physics-hz 100 --control-hz 20 --disable-self-collisions \
  --idle-stop-s 0
```

Use `Ctrl-C` in the pipeline or create the declared `--stop-file` to end it
gracefully; evidence is written before Isaac shutdown. This is not yet a T007
pass: inspect the recorded arm/head targets, observed joint states, hold events
and video, then compare the exact raw trace offline before claiming equivalence.
Collision remains inconclusive under the asset workaround.

The controller gate accepts either TeleVuer's digital trigger flag or its
validated analog pull value at or below `5.0` on the wrapper's inverted
`10=released, 0=fully pressed` scale. This fallback is necessary because the
2026-08-17 Quest session delivered poses while leaving both digital trigger
flags false. Every effective press/release transition, the raw boolean, and the
analog value are recorded in the bridge JSONL. `--idle-stop-s 0` prevents the
simulator process from terminating during a recoverable WebXR pose gap; the
mapper still enters `command_timeout` hold, and the operator stops the bounded
run with `Ctrl-C` or its stop file.

## Initial live outcome and protocol revision — 2026-08-11

`t007_live_arm_head_20260811T074737Z` completed with no received command and
is retained as an execution/connection observation. The subsequent live run,
`t007_live_arm_head_20260811T080127Z`, received 4,753 normalized commands.
Fresh-command age was 0.53–40.14 ms (mean 16.85 ms), so this run does not show
that the host/Quest transport itself is the primary realtime bottleneck.

It did reveal a protocol limitation: only 578 arm/head dispatches were
accepted, while 1,457 holds came from leaving the selected workspace envelope
and 114 from IK refusal; other holds were deadman and timeout events. This is
consistent with the observed failure to follow large extension motions, and is
not a valid claim of full-workspace tracking.

After that run, the editable T007 config was revised from position scale 1.0 to
0.3 and the command protocol gained an explicit operator lifecycle: a rising
left-trigger edge returns the simulated arms/head to the declared neutral pose
and clears the calibration; the next right-trigger command captures a fresh
relative neutral. The two initial runs use the old no-reset/scale-1.0/free-root
behavior and are retained but are not comparable to a run made under the
revised config. The next live run must be assessed separately from its own
resolved configuration and calibration event record.

## Workspace-prefilter revision — 2026-08-11

`t007_fixed_base_20260811T084211Z` verified the fixed root but still recorded
151 holds from the legacy rectangular prefilter, before IK could test those
targets. The editable live profile now uses `workspace_policy=ik_joint_limit_only`
(`enforce_workspace=false`): it removes that prefilter but retains the IK hard
joint limits, convergence checks, deadman and timeout holds. A solution that
converges exactly at a position-joint limit is now dispatched and explicitly
recorded; it is not treated as an IK failure. The current schema-2 profile
clamps a roll request beyond its wrist limit and records that status. This is a
simulation-only policy, not a hardware policy.
This is a protocol compatibility break. All earlier T007 runs remain immutable
legacy observations; the next run is the first evidence for the new policy.

## Configuration-snapshot correction — 2026-08-11

The runner initially stored only the mutable pathname of the T007 arm/head
profile, while `experiment_config.json` contained the shared T001 bridge
config. From the next run onward, `experiment_config.json` is the immutable T007
profile, `bridge_config.json` snapshots the bridge configuration, and both
hashes are in `metadata.json`. `t007_ik_full_reach_20260811T085504Z` is retained
as an observed functional run but is not a reproducible quantitative baseline
because it predates this correction.

The same correction adds requested target position, solver status, residual,
limit margin and clamped-joint names to every later `ik_refused` event. It makes
the next run suitable for identifying whether a missed extension was genuinely
outside the arm workspace, a roll-limit request, or a solver-basin failure.

## R1-A5 endpoint and absolute-mapping correction — 2026-08-17

Inspection of `t007_fixed_base_20260811T084211Z` and the vendored R1-A5 IK
identified three coupled method errors. The old T007 profile used a 0.3 motion
scale; its declared neutral solved to about -0.716 rad elbow instead of joint
zero; and the local solver controlled the wrist joint origin while the vendor
R1-A5 IK defines its virtual end-effector 0.20 m along wrist local +x. After
adding that tool offset, the first implementation also exposed a Jacobian bug:
the Jacobian still differentiated the wrist origin rather than the corrected
end-effector.

Schema 2 fixes all four points: FK and Jacobian use the same vendor endpoint,
reset writes the declared q=0 pose directly, arm motion is absolute vendor pose at scale 1:1,
and unreachable position targets are explicitly projected to the bounded joint
workspace instead of freezing both arms. This invalidates all earlier T002-T007
workspace/IK results for comparison with new runs; they remain preserved as
legacy evidence.

The first schema-2 Isaac smoke exposed one additional startup artifact: the
articulation still spawned in its bent default joint pose even though reset q=0
was declared. The runner now writes q=0 to both arms/head before the control
loop and synchronizes the IK seeds and rate limiters to it. This change applies
only to runs created after 2026-08-17.

## Live schema-2 analysis and rate revision — 2026-08-17

`t007_live_arm_head_20260817T114118Z` is the first complete live run after the
endpoint, startup-pose and trigger corrections. It completed by operator
`SIGINT`, recorded all required artifacts and video, received 3,150 valid
commands, dispatched 1,634 bilateral arm/head targets, and never dispatched
base velocity. The articulation root displacement and velocity were both
exactly zero. Trigger transitions show right deadman and both left-trigger
reset edges were received through the revised digital/analog input path.

This run remains `scientific_outcome=unassessed`: almost half of the enabled
samples were outside exact joint-limited reach (716 left, 735 right). Mean IK
position residual was 0.0242 m left and 0.0322 m right, with maxima 0.1956 m
and 0.2389 m. The larger visible mismatch came after IK: requested endpoint to
rate-limited command was 0.1276/0.1313 m mean and 0.5163/0.4868 m p95; command
to observed articulation was 0.0502/0.0489 m mean and approximately 0.089 m
p95. Therefore loosening the geometric workspace or changing posture bias
would not address the dominant lag.

An offline replay over the exact target trace kept the IK and posture method
fixed and varied only the simulation rate pair. Changing from `1.0 rad/s`,
`2.0 rad/s²` to `1.5 rad/s`, `4.0 rad/s²` reduced expected mean
requested-to-command error to 0.0778/0.0802 m and p95 to 0.3422/0.3154 m.
Higher pairs reduced replay error further but were not selected: the requested
change was modest, and replay does not include any resulting articulation lag.
The editable config now carries the selected pair and its source. This is a
protocol parameter revision, not an observed improvement; a new live run must
verify smoothness and post-physics tracking before replacing the old result.

New runs also record pre-limiter IK joint targets and limiter velocities, so
future figures can separate IK residual, rate-limit lag and articulation lag
without reconstructing hidden solver state. The plotter now draws requested,
rate-limited-command and observed endpoints with all three error components.
